# Editorial Rereads

Reread the article three times. Keep the passes separate so polish does not hide a structural problem.

## 1. Author presence

- What happened, and why did this author care?
- Where did the author's feeling, uncertainty, humor, or judgment change?
- Does the first person carry experience, or only announce conclusions?
- Could this passage appear unchanged under another author's name?
- Did research or a useful framework push the author out of the article?

For a thinking article, missing lived presence is a `REBUILD`, not a line-editing problem.

## 2. Movement and reader

- What invites the reader in?
- Where does the article genuinely turn?
- Which sections merely prove a conclusion already reached?
- What should be cut even though it is correct or polished?
- Does the ending return to reality, leave an honest question, or make a real choice?

Do not reward completeness, symmetry, citation count, frameworks, or tidy closure.

## 3. Fact and safety

- No invented or unapproved author/third-party material.
- Facts, quotations, dates, and attributions are accurate.
- Sources support the adjacent claims.
- Observation, interpretation, and external fact remain distinguishable.
- Privacy, front matter, links, media, and publishing boundaries hold.

## Verdict

Use:

```text
KEEP | REBUILD | KILL

Living center:
Where the author disappears:
Where the article turns:
Cut first:
Fact/privacy issue:
Unresolved human choice:
```

`KEEP` means the shape is alive and safe. `REBUILD` means the material deserves another shape. `KILL` means further polish would only manufacture importance.
