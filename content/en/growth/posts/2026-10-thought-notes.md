### Anxiety is the same

Then things changed. I began worrying about why I was anxious, whether anxiety would affect tomorrow, whether I had been doing badly lately… One word dragged the whole night into it

What would happen if I did not call it anxiety? If I just described the present?

My chest is a little tight. My breathing is shallow. A thought about tomorrow's meeting keeps coming up, repeatedly asking what would happen if I made a fool of myself

Just these three things. Once I said them, they became smaller instead. If my chest is tight, take a few deep breaths. Write the thought down and leave it until tomorrow morning. This process is seeing rather than defining. Often, many emotions only need to be seen and written down clearly; looking at them after some time, they resolve themselves

Psychology has research on affect labeling. [A 2007 experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/) found reduced responses in some brain regions when participants named emotions in negative images. That gives me one way to understand my experience, but does not mean every emotion will resolve simply by being put into words.

Zhuangzi says to forget the fish trap once you have caught the fish. A name is the tool for catching fish, not the fish itself. Buddhism also says that what we call “I am very anxious,” broken down, is merely some sensations and thoughts coming together at this moment and dispersing in the next

My method is that when I notice an emotion, good or negative, my first reaction is anticipation, because emotions help me understand myself. Every moment of being moved, disappointed or anxious seems to help me grow if I see it

Then reconstruct the emotion: what exactly happened in this moment?

If, during the day, I also have to think about how to list my itinerary and how to arrange my plans, that behavior itself consumes energy

Then I suddenly thought of Eason. In managing his daily life, he uses a really brilliant word, a really brilliant concept: bottleneck. It is a word you can keep thinking about. You can ask: what is the bottleneck? What is the bottleneck in the thing you are trying to do right now? It is very clear, a very specific thing and a very specific intention. Concepts are also the basic units of thought, so every day you can think outward from this concept and work around it. Then each day has a clear main thread. It is not sprawling or fragmented; it is one clear thread: solving the bottleneck in the thing you are working on now, and thinking about how to solve it. What methods do you use in the process? You focus all your attention, energy, learning and time on this concept

And a bottleneck is something you can keep thinking about. Once you solve the current bottleneck, you can keep asking: what is the next bottleneck? What is the next bottleneck?

I have suddenly had a realization. I think that in the future AI era, when facing complex tasks and events, especially as an individual who has a lot of work to do, the bottleneck is a very good method. It is more effective than all those energy management methods, time management methods and task lists. Every day you need to think clearly and define clearly: what is the current problem? What is the most central bottleneck? Keep thinking about and solving things around that bottleneck. Then every day you can clearly say what you did to address it

### Many people approach love as a finite game, including in East Asian culture

> 2026-10-09 15:35:24

Many people approach love as a finite game. East Asian culture also treats love this way, mistaking the establishment of a relationship for a permanent identity. Especially in modern society, love is highly structured, with a series of milestones: a crush, a confession, becoming a couple, moving in together, getting engaged, getting married

It seems to have become part of East Asian culture: proceeding step by step toward an endpoint, on a journey that can be judged a success or failure. Even our phrases suggest it: “reaching fruition,” “ending as well as it began”

But I think the essence of love should be closer to an infinite game

By the definition in Finite and Infinite Games, an infinite game is played to keep the game going. Rules can change, the framework can change; the purpose is continuation itself

So winning someone's heart does not mean you have won. An argument does not have to produce a winner and loser, and marriage does not have to be the finish line. In a relationship, keeping score of who gives more or less, who loses more or less, makes it meaningless

But placing it within an infinite game makes the game itself interesting: both people keep growing. You argue to let the relationship continue and work in a healthy, better way, rather than to defeat the other person

The rules keep being redefined as the two people change. They are not pursuing an endpoint, nor are they held by past sunk costs, such as “a ten-year relationship.” Sunk costs do not enter the decision

Although this is an idealized concept, I think the most moving moments in love are when two people keep growing and being seen, changing the rules together, writing their shared story and experiencing life!

### How to collect user stories:

> 2026-10-04 23:05:06

Every valid user story corresponds to a set of eval cases. ### Choosing an approach to generative UI: experience and thoughts

> 2026-10-11 11:48:55

Compose components: developers write the components and actions first; the model generates JSON to decide their composition and data binding. The style is consistent and the testing scope clear, making this suitable for dynamic forms and dashboards. Its ceiling depends on the component catalog; new interactions still need development. See AG-UI

Generate programs: the model writes HTML or JavaScript on the spot and runs it in an isolated environment. It can create previously unknown simulators and small tools; Claude and Gemini have public implementations. The trade-off is less predictable generation time and results. You need to check calculation logic, handle runtime errors, and preserve code versions and user state. See Gemini's dynamic view

Embed existing applications: the model selects tools and opens interfaces developers have already written. This suits task boards and editors, reusing mature business capabilities. ### Published on LangChain's official blog on October 14

> 2026-10-15 10:27:02

LangChain's official blog published [Context Engineering: How Deep Agents Remember](https://www.langchain.com/blog/context-engineering-deep-agents-remember) on October 14, by Jess Weng. It discusses context rot in long-running agents: the longer the task, the more instructions, tool results and intermediate drafts pile up, and the less accurate the model becomes. Deep Agents uses five methods to move this content out of the active window without losing it:

Offload large tool results: save them in files, leaving only a preview in context.

Summarize: create a summary when approaching the threshold, while keeping the original retrievable.

Load skills on demand: add them to context only when needed.

Delegate to subagents: let subagents handle noisy work.

Store long-term memory as Markdown files: under your own control.

What surprised me most was offloading: putting the whole pile into a cabinet unchanged, that is, saving it as a file, and leaving only a sticky note on the desk saying what it is, which file it is in, and what the opening lines look like. Later, when a particular section is needed, the agent uses read or grep to retrieve exactly that section. To me, it is much like human working memory, because our working memory is itself very small

There are already fairly good engineering practices and examples for the other approaches. But I find this working-memory idea interesting. It resembles the earlier hot.md, though it actually solves a different problem. Specifically:

Hot.md keeps a small amount of key information permanently in context. Offload moves large amounts out of context and brings them back only when needed.

Hot.md holds concise rules, preferences and progress, within a few hundred lines. Offload holds long, messy raw tool results, such as logs, web pages and search results.

Hot.md is carefully maintained by a person or agent, with repeated rewriting. Offload is handled automatically by the framework; the original is written once and rarely changed.

Hot.md persists across sessions. Offloaded files are mostly used only for the current task and can be discarded afterward

Implementing offload:

Add middleware at the tool-call layer: when a result exceeds a threshold, say 20k tokens, write it to /tmp/results/.md.

Return a stub to the model: file path, total length, and the first N lines or an automatically generated summary.

Equip the agent with tools such as read_file(path, offset, limit) and grep, so it can read sections on demand.

Optionally, clear these files when the task ends, or distill valuable conclusions into long-term memory such as Hot.md.

### Your AI assistant might be picking the wrong tool

> 2026-10-17 09:46:36

[Your AI assistant might be picking the wrong tool](https://medium.com/@mehmetselim692/your-ai-assistant-is-picking-the-wrong-tool-and-nothing-tells-you-bce3a9357624)

When tool descriptions are unclear, an Agent can easily select the wrong tool. The mistake does not raise an error; it quietly produces worse results. The author built an open-source tool, mispick: it reads an MCP server's tool list, creates requests for each tool that are easy to confuse with neighboring tools, asks a real model to select repeatedly, and reports which tools are most often confused and each tool's accuracy.

More than the tool itself, I think its experimental approach is worth learning from. It has roughly five steps:

Read the tool list and obtain each tool's name, description and parameter schema. Together, these form the input the model uses to select a tool

Build tricky questions from this information. These are not easy questions: for each tool, generate requests at its boundary with neighboring tools, specifically to test whether the model confuses them

Give the full tool list and questions to a real model. Record which tool it selects and how many times each question is run, since model output is stochastic

Summarize the results in a confusion matrix. Rows are the correct answers and columns the selected tools, immediately showing which correct tools are often mistaken for which others

Calculate precision and recall from this, with confidence intervals

I think this offers inspiration for future evaluations: how to deliberately construct data and obtain more realistic precision and recall

Risk tiers: read = automatic; draft = automatic; external write / pay / delete = human required. Use a policy table rather than relying on the model's discretion.

A unified Action Proposal: {action, target, preview, reversible?, risk}. The UI renders only this kind of card (Duet's “show you first, then wait” belongs at this layer).

Classifier / auto-mode: a model or separate classifier decides whether automation is allowed; high-risk actions force an interruption (the approach used by Claude Code / Duet's auto-mode classifier).

Idempotency + reversibility: draft when possible rather than sending live; soft-delete when possible rather than hard-delete; approval timeout = cancellation, never silent sending.

Approval outside the conversation: a notification opens just that card for approval, without returning to a long conversation. Essential for proactive scenarios.

Audit log: who approved what and when, allowing replay if something goes wrong

List all tools with side effects and mark auto / ask / never

Every ask needs a plain-language preview, rather than JSON alone.

### sandbox is first of all about execution

> 2026-10-04 00:04:33

sandbox is first of all about execution in a computer environment. Essentially, it is needed because untrusted execution is taking place on a computer

So if database CRUD is already executed by a controlled backend, each structured call usually does not need its own separate code-execution sandbox

Reading files from S3 does not need a sandbox either, provided these really are structured tools executed by the backend on your behalf. Their obvious features are that parameters can be validated and permissions controlled down to particular rows and users

Even pure file reading and writing does not actually need a sandbox. You can simply provide read file and write file tools, with the backend reading directly under the user's prefix. It is no different from CRUD. The key is whether bash is involved

A sandbox is needed when running shell commands: executing code written by the model, installing dependencies, opening a browser, or using commands like GREP and sed to process a pile of files. These require an execution environment

High-frequency actions must become structured tools. Even if a sandbox can do them, there is no need; for greater speed and precision, and to avoid crossing permission boundaries


Not opening a separate execution sandbox does not mean omitting authentication, input validation, data isolation or auditing. The distinction here concerns execution methods, not whether security boundaries are needed.
