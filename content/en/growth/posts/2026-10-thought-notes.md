### How to collect user stories:

> 2026-10-04 23:05:06

Two layers. The first is raw material, with as little friction as possible: user interviews, screenshots of group chats, moments when I get stuck using the product, and screen recordings. Throw them all into a raw material area first, simply labeled with the date and source

The second is distillation. Each week, pick recurring stories from the material and make each one a row in the database, with the type set to “User story.” Use four fixed sections in the body: who, in what situation, what they want to accomplish, and where they are stuck now. Always attach a link to the original material at the end

Then comes evaluation, which is actually related to the second layer of user stories

Every valid user story corresponds to a set of eval cases. The story describes “what the user wants”; the cases verify “whether we delivered it.” In Notion, keep only the eval design and conclusions: what is being evaluated, the passing criteria, a summary of each run's results, the corresponding commit, and the judgment reached. Keep the cases themselves and scoring data in the repository or eval platform, with only links in Notion. Once the volume of data grows, nobody will maintain it in Notion

There are all sorts of methods and tools for managing time and energy online too. But in actual practice, I still find it troublesome, because management itself is a burden. If, during the day, I also have to think about how to list my itinerary and how to arrange my plans, that behavior itself consumes energy

Then I suddenly thought of Eason. In managing his daily life, he uses a really brilliant word, a really brilliant concept: bottleneck. It is a word you can keep thinking about. You can ask: what is the bottleneck? What is the bottleneck in the thing you are trying to do right now? It is very clear, a very specific thing and a very specific intention. Concepts are also the basic units of thought, so every day you can think outward from this concept and work around it. Then each day has a clear main thread. It is not sprawling or fragmented; it is one clear thread: solving the bottleneck in the thing you are working on now, and thinking about how to solve it. What methods do you use in the process? You focus all your attention, energy, learning and time on this concept

And a bottleneck is something you can keep thinking about. Once you solve the current bottleneck, you can keep asking: what is the next bottleneck? What is the next bottleneck?

I have suddenly had a realization. I think that in the future AI era, when facing complex tasks and events, especially as an individual who has a lot of work to do, the bottleneck is a very good method. It is more effective than all those energy management methods, time management methods and task lists. Every day you need to think clearly and define clearly: what is the current problem? What is the most central bottleneck? Keep thinking about and solving things around that bottleneck. Then every day you can clearly say what you did to address it

### forgetting is more important than memory

> 2026-10-07 11:23:14

In real agent work, people find that the problems gradually point toward Memory. Memory is also the hardest thing to build evaluation for

total recall is actually a burden. On one hand, the model's intelligence can easily decline as context explodes. On the other, misleading information in the context may cause an LLM to hold firmly to mistaken beliefs

So a small amount of accurate information works far better than complete information mixed with a pile of garbage signals

There are many mature solutions for memory online! But for forgetting, there are three criteria in a business setting:

If the source of information is not very reliable, it does not count and should not be trusted

Some information is time-sensitive, such as information about a particular event or about an event I attended

Withdraw it immediately when an error is discovered. An agent that cannot retract mistakes only sinks deeper into them

These three points are also very helpful for building evaluation. Establishing criteria for what should be remembered is very difficult; it depends heavily on human subjectivity

But we can make a very clear judgment about certain things that should not be recorded, should be corrected, or should have a time limit

In fact, this is how the world works

### Use design to align taste

> 2026-10-01 23:58:44

Define colors, font sizes, spacing, corner radii, shadows and animation durations as a set of variables (design tokens). Every page may use only these variables

Use a mature system: React + tailwind

Base the components mostly on unstyled primitive libraries such as shadcn/ui and Radix. The foundation already handles buttons, dialogs, dropdown menus, keyboard shortcuts and accessibility; the team only needs to define its own style on top

Do a UI review regularly to find problems in the details

Write the design specification as a rules file (such as AGENTS.md or .cursor/rules), stating: “Use only these components and tokens; do not hardcode colors.” Have a design engineer polish AI's first draft. This keeps things fast without becoming increasingly disorganized

For every change, combine the relevant components with chromatic visual regression testing to prevent drift

Elegant products usually invest in these areas: smooth transition animations (150 to 250 milliseconds, often using Framer Motion), skeleton screens and streaming output (especially important for AI products, to avoid idle waiting), complete keyboard shortcuts and a Cmd+K command palette, dark mode, and dedicated designs for empty states and error states
