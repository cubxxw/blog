### The Free China Movement

> 2025-11-05 15:29:24 · `#格物/外交`

In the early Cold War (around the early 1950s), a secret anti-communist operation against the People's Republic of China (the mainland) led by the US Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) - the "Free China Movement" (also called the "Free China Resistance Movement" or "Asian Resistance Movement") and its affiliated agent training activities

At the time the US was carrying out a large-scale anti-communist deployment in Asia, providing resources and training

Participants came mainly from Hong Kong, including intellectuals concerned about the nation (who couldn't accept leadership by either the Communist Party or Chiang Kai-shek), young Nationalist officers and soldiers from Rennie's Mill (Tiu Keng Leng), and out-of-school, unemployed youth. They joined out of political conviction or basic livelihood needs

The goal was "to build a beautiful 'Free China'". The specific task was airdropping into the mainland, that is, dispatching trained guerrillas to infiltrate the mainland to conduct anti-communist guerrilla warfare, intelligence collection and other secret activities, hoping to subvert or weaken the PRC regime

The training site was Saipan (the "Saipan cadre school"). Trainees there underwent one year and two months of guerrilla warfare, demolition, parachuting and other agent training

The mode of operation was four-man airdrop teams, choosing the guerrillas' hometowns as drop points, intending to use the familiar environment to operate

Before airdrop missions, each graduate from Saipan was issued a pistol, a submachine gun, ammunition, a radio transmitter, enough dry rations for a month, and renminbi; then they were secretly flown to the mountains of some province, and jumped

Some people's parachutes didn't open and they died on the spot. Most people, as soon as they landed, were tied up by local residents and sent off to be shot

On November 29, 1952, a CIA C-47 transport plane on a resupply mission to an airdropped team was shot down by PLA troops lying in ambush; the pilot and navigator died on the spot, and two Americans who parachuted out were captured: instructors at the CIA's Atsugi spy training facility in Japan, John T. Downey and Richard George Fecteau. It caused a diplomatic conflict between China and the US, and the program was terminated

### Human behavior analyzed from a systems-theory perspective

> 2025-11-11 15:32:24 · `#格物/系统学`

A system is an organism in which the whole is greater than the sum of its parts, with core features such as emergence, feedback loops, self-organization and openness. Applying systems theory to human behavior patterns, the individual can be seen as an open Complex Adaptive System (CAS)

Human behavior patterns themselves can be abstracted as a dynamic loop system of input - process - output - feedback

Input: external stimuli (such as social pressure, environmental changes) and internal needs (such as hunger, emotion)

Throughput: processing by subsystems, including the physiological system (nerves, endocrine), the psychological system (cognition, emotion) and the behavioral system (habits, decisions)

Output: observable behavior patterns, such as repeated habits, social interaction or avoidance responses

Feedback:

Positive Feedback: amplifies change, promotes innovation or crisis (such as the addiction loop: taking drugs → pleasure → taking drugs again)

Negative Feedback: maintains homeostasis, such as hunger → eating → fullness → stop eating

The emergence and wholeness of systems: behavior patterns "emerge" from the interaction of subsystems and cannot simply be reduced to a sum of parts. For example, procrastination isn't simply "laziness" (psychology), but an overall pattern of fear (emotion subsystem) + immediate reward (positive feedback) + low self-esteem (social feedback)

Equifinality and multifinality

Equifinality: the same goal can be reached through multiple paths (such as losing weight: diet OR exercise OR drugs)

Multifinality: the same starting point can lead to different outcomes (such as childhood adversity: some become entrepreneurs, some become depressed — depending on the strength of the feedback loop)

Human systems form stable patterns through self-organization, such as habit formation (Hebbian learning: neurons "fire together, wire together"). In complex environments, behavior shows an Edge of Chaos: moderate disturbance promotes adaptation (such as innovation in a crisis), while too much causes collapse (such as burnout)

### Priests among the Maasai

> 2025-11-25 11:19:15 · `#格物/马赛人`

The Maasai's traditional priest is called a Laibon

Laibons usually come from specific family lineages (several of the most famous Laibon families are well known in both Kenya and Tanzania, such as the Ngosorongi family in Tanzania).

The criterion for validation is spiritual power (orngo), believed to be passed down through bloodline

Usually the previous Laibon designates a successor on his deathbed (often his own son or grandson), and only after long training and secret rituals does that person formally take office

Where it overlaps heavily with Christian priests:

communicating with the supreme god Enkai — the most important mediator between humans and god.

performing blessing rituals for the tribe or family (praying for rain, for livestock fertility, pre-war blessings, healing illness).

presiding over major life rituals: birth naming, circumcision (coming-of-age for both men and women), marriage, and funerals.

prophesying the future, interpreting dreams, divining (using stones, shoes, entrails, etc.).

healing illness (herbs + spells + ritual, combining the double role of medicine man and priest).

providing oracles in tribal war or major decisions, with influence that sometimes exceeds the chief's.

Where it's most similar to a Christian priest:

people go to him first when in trouble (like going to church to find a padre).

he gives people "blessings" (smearing saliva on livestock, smearing a milk-and-honey mixture on people).

he can "absolve sins" or perform purification rituals (for example, if someone killed a fellow clansman, a Laibon must perform a ritual before the tribe accepts them back).

he keeps sacred objects (similar to holy relics), and holds collective prayer ceremonies.

Where it differs from a Christian priest:

The Laibon is at once diviner, medicine man, and prophet — more powerful, more mysterious, even fearsome.

They can have multiple wives and live relatively well off (the tribe gives many cattle and sheep as payment).

There's no fixed "church"; rituals usually take place under a sacred tree, on a particular hilltop, or in the center of the village.

There are no written scriptures — everything relies on oral transmission and memory

Current situation: many Maasai have converted to Christianity (Catholicism, Presbyterianism, Pentecostalism, etc.), and fewer and fewer in the younger generation are willing to inherit the Laibon role

But some famous Laibon families are still very active, and today's Laibon often has to "compete" with Christian pastors: when someone gets sick, they'll go to the church pastor to pray and also to the Laibon to slaughter a cow and perform a ritual
