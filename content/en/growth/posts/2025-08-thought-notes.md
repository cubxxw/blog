### Understanding persona

> 2025-08-02 11:16:09

Persona refers to the image a person deliberately crafts in public.

The reason we take such pains to play a "pleasant person," a "formidable person," or an "interesting person" is that deep down we long for others' approval, liking, and acceptance. We're afraid that if we don't, we'll be "disliked."

So what's decided here is whether we should actively cater to others, carrying a boulder uphill by ourselves.

The solution is "the courage to be disliked."

There's one very core method: separation of tasks, first principles.

Why do we go build a persona: we pursue approval, we want to please others.

Why pursuing approval is painful: because it's someone else's task, and we've taken it as our own.

This involves separation of tasks:

My task: express myself sincerely, work hard, live well.

Others' task: seeing my words and actions, disliking me or liking me.

The essence of "persona" is that we try to use "my task" (playing a certain role) to control "others' task" (others' view of me). Adler tells us this is futile, and it's the root of suffering.

That way you don't need to worry about being criticized on Xiaohongshu; focus on your own task and leave the right to judge to others.

### Kick-the-cat effect

> 2025-08-10 15:42:13

If the transmission of emotion could be described in one phrase, it would be a psychological phenomenon: the kick-the-cat effect.

It means a person, having received bad emotions, pressure, or unfair treatment from a superior or the outside world, can't vent it directly at the source, so instead vents that emotion onto people or things weaker than themselves or less able to resist.

The boss criticizes an employee at the company → the employee goes home in a bad mood → takes it out on their partner → the partner yells at the child → the child feels wronged and goes to kick the family cat.

They usually pick someone or something weaker than themselves that can't fight back.

It reminds me of a friend I met who was oppressed by their boss — which actually came from the boss's own unhappy family of origin.

### The essence of parents' nagging: the sense-of-control hypothesis

> 2025-08-12 11:59:43 · `#人生/成长`

I suddenly came across a description of this concept, and suddenly I could understand why parents repeat over and over things I already know, they know I know, and they even don't know why they say them.

### Why do humans value endings so much, and why do humans also care so much about the present

> 2025-08-14 17:14:08 · `#人生/成长`

Perfect endings vs. cherishing the present.

People are influenced by environment, by human evolutionary mechanisms, by the modern fixation on a perfect future — because the happiness we imagine tends to be terminal, and we always overemphasize results rather than process,

but it also seems like a conflict between delayed gratification and instant gratification.

Delayed gratification is in a way like craving — hoping for something better. People overestimate future rewards, which leads to endless deferral of gratification, while rarely truly living in the present.

But usually in the process of pursuing a goal, the brain rewards and produces anticipatory pleasure, yet once it's achieved, it's often less satisfying than expected (the "peak-end rule": people remember the climax and the ending more than the process). This explains why things easily obtained aren't cherished: without the effort process, there's no sense of achievement.

Plus some cultural influence: film and literature reinforce perfect endings, making people live in fantasy rather than the present, and the result is anxiety.

Japanese cultural aesthetics like "wabi-sabi" praise imperfection and transience, and promote ichigo ichie as a "healing" philosophy: the feast won't come again, so live in the present. This isn't a human instinct.

On another level, there are certain scenarios in which people return to the present:

Moments of crisis and awakening, letting go of the pursuit of endings and of anxiety.

Old age or late life — is it a lowering of craving? More about liking, liking the present, a peaceful life.

Meditation, travel, practice, art, deep relationships — these also make it easy to enter a "flow state" and forget the ending.

When uncertainty is too high, instinct eases the anxiety.

Returning to the present often needs an external trigger or inner training; it's not instinct, it's learning to "accept uncertainty."

### The brain's dopamine is often most active not only when you get satisfied

> 2025-08-14 17:56:25 · `#人生/成长`

It suddenly occurs to me why humans have delayed gratification, and why humans pursue perfect endings.

In fact, the dopamine system already delivers a very rich reward at the moment of anticipation or craving, which is why we're often extremely happy before we look forward to good food or to travel.

So dopamine has two very core aspects:

Wanting: dopamine-driven. Every time you scroll down, you feel satisfied; this also has a reward — a reward of uncertainty and anticipation.

Liking: actually seeing a fantastic video, or tasting something very delicious — that pure sense of joy.

So in a way, anticipation is also a process of releasing dopamine. "Anticipation" is the cause, "wanting" is the effect.

As a person grows up, the wanting system tends to get lower and lower, and anticipation actually drops too. When you're young, people are more driven by craving to chase goals; but with age, a lot of the craving goes away.

So it makes sense that young people also get addicted to short videos more easily, and to gambling, haha. Young people's dopamine systems are in a much more active state, pursuing exploration and new experience. For older people, the surprises gradually come from content, or from some deeper hobbies and meaningful activities.

### Lowering expectations: thinking deeply once more

> 2025-08-14 18:03:22 · `#人生/成长`

A topic we discussed back in Dali: "Slowly I lost my expectations, and instead had more happiness."

At the dopamine "liking" level, lowering expectations makes it easier to feel satisfied with the current state — this is what we call contentment brings happiness; you won't feel disappointment or anxiety because of the gap between expectation and reality.

But on the other hand, achievement and excitement: lowering expectations actually strips away an important source of happiness, because the enormous joy and peak experience of setting a challenging goal and finally achieving it through effort are produced jointly by strong "wanting" (anticipation) and the ultimate "liking" (achievement).

Lowering expectations — really lowering your psychological expectations — is a very smart strategy, because it's easier to exceed your psychological expectation, and the excess is easily read by the brain as a surprise, releasing dopamine. But life is also a balance between craving and contentment.

So the key isn't lowering expectations, or having no expectations, but managing expectations.

### The essence of communication

> 2025-08-14 23:56:13

There are actually two parts: the content level and the relationship level. Think about which level the other person's purpose is at.

If it's content, you should focus on discussing the content.

If it's relationship, then it's a different path:

The other person's intent is mostly not "wanting the right answer," but wanting their emotion to be seen or the relational order to be confirmed.

Your ability is to give a rational explanation, but if the way you express it doesn't match what the other person is "asking for," it wears down the relationship and your energy.

First let the other person be seen, then discuss right and wrong, cost, and solutions.

Accept: first catch the emotion (which doesn't mean agreeing with the view).

Negotiate: shift the topic from "right or wrong" to "choices/cost/division of labor."

Refuse: clear boundary + friendly tone + offering an alternative (if willing).

Explaining satisfies a logical need; catching satisfies a relational need.

When the other person "wants relationship, not solutions," give the relationship first, and the problem more easily enters the logical dimension. Get the order right and the cost is low.

Some general thoughts on explaining, when it's aimed at relationship:

First let them be seen, and only then will they be heard.

Relationship before logic.

Facts instead of arguments; a viewpoint can also be a small experiment.

Boundary = clear + friendly + optional.

Your optimal decision depends on your judgment of the other's decision, and the other's optimal decision also depends on his judgment of your decision. What it studies isn't isolated optimal decisions (like how Robinson Crusoe allocates resources on a desert island), but situations where multiple decision-makers influence and act on each other.

The other's decision affects your decision, your decision affects the other's — this is where two-player games begin, and then multi-player games follow.

Games have a purpose.

Two different types of games:

Zero-sum game: you lose, I win

Positive-sum game: win-win (the importance of communication, understanding, trust)

### The foundation of reinforcement learning is probability theory: the Markov Decision Process (MDP)

> 2025-08-05 14:51:52

Think of it as the rulebook you read before playing a game.

MDP is a method that looks only at the present, ignores the past, and cycles repeatedly through choice–result–feedback.

Essentially it is:

S (state): the robot's position

A (action): up, down, left, right

P (transition probability): the rule that the ground is very slippery

R (reward): the score for each step

Once the rules are set, the harder part is how to play. That way of playing is the policy.

Whenever the robot enters a new state (lands on a new square), the policy tells it which action to take next.

And if the robot follows this optimal policy, the expected total return it can get in each state is the Optimal Value Function, written V.

The Bellman Optimality Equation — reinforcement learning's dragon-slaying technique.

"The optimal value of a place equals the immediate benefit you get after making the smartest choice, plus the expectation of the future optimal value of all the places you might go next."

The beauty of this equation is that it simplifies a "global optimality" problem spanning the whole journey into a local relationship involving only "now" and "the next step."

### How serums, moisturizers, creams, and toners actually work

> 2025-08-14 14:48:05

I'm generally curious. I thought serums were needed in many situations, but actually you need to understand how they work.

I feel that memorizing things before wasn't very meaningful, because my memory is too poor — I'm better at understanding than memorizing.

The logic of the skin barrier is a "brick wall structure": the bricks are corneocytes (containing keratin and NMF, natural moisturizing factor), and the mortar is lipids (ceramides/cholesterol/free fatty acids in a layered bimolecular film).

Transepidermal water loss (TEWL). Water diffuses from the dermis toward the epidermis → evaporates; the more stable the barrier, the lower the TEWL, and the less dry the skin.

So one is serum: a water-based solution / gel, raising the effective concentration of actives reaching the skin's site of action. It follows something like Fick's law of diffusion — the larger the concentration gradient, the more suitable the carrier, the more "friendly" the stratum corneum, the more effective penetration (mostly it stays in the upper epidermis / near the granular layer, and very rarely goes "as deep as the blood").

So the purpose of a serum is generally antioxidant, anti-aging, moisturizing and repairing.

The purpose of a toner is generally a water-based, low-oil or oil-free formula emphasizing hydration and texture conditioning.

It's generally for quickly restoring water and adjusting pH. In a dry air-conditioned environment it's needed, but if your cleanser is gentle and your skin is stable, toner is optional.

Cream does a lot: it's the main "sealing layer" for moisturizing and barrier repair, and it can also deliver oil-soluble actives.

At night you can usually apply a thicker layer; during the day, to avoid pilling and stuffiness, you can choose a lighter one.

So sunscreen generally provides light protection on the outermost layer, forming a "chemical + physical" double insurance with morning antioxidants.

So whether it's serum or hydration, if you don't use cream, it's as if you did nothing, haha.

### Some health knowledge about drinking water

> 2025-08-25 20:05:08

You must drink water when you get up early, preferably warm water. After a night of the body consuming itself you're in a dehydrated state; this glass is life-saving water.

Also, drinking water 30 minutes before a meal increases satiety and avoids overeating.

During exercise, replenish water in small amounts, frequently.

Drinking water before and after a shower is important too, to avoid losing water from showering.

When you feel tired, or can't concentrate, it's usually a signal your body is short of water.

One or two hours before sleep, drink a small glass of water.

### Do drinks have any value?

> 2025-08-25 20:10:03

Cola has almost no nutritional value; occasionally as a "happy water" it's fine.

Milk has high value, drink more.

Packaged juice is really not recommended — it's about the same as sugar water, since almost all the fruit fiber is lost in processing.

Freshly squeezed orange juice might have some use, but it also loses a lot of fiber.

Packaged juice and fresh juice are very different.

Even "100% pure juice" has already lost a lot of flavor and nutrition.

Only juice labeled NFC is not-from-concentrate, but it's much more expensive, and still no match for fresh juice.
